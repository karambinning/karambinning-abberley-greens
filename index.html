What's in, what's useful.

No two days look the same on the shelves — what we stock follows what the street wants. The list below is the everyday core. Anything missing, ask at the counter and we'll add it to the next order.

— from the till
Fresh & loose
day to day

Potatoes, onions, root veg, salad bits, lemons, garlic, bananas, top-of-the-fruit-bowl apples. Brought in small, sold by the piece — no shrink-wrap unless it earns it.

Bread & milk
every morning

Sliced and unsliced loaves, baps, rolls. Whole, semi, skimmed and the blue-top your tea wants. Butter, eggs and the bits a fry-up actually needs.

Papers
early doors

The dailies, the locals, magazines on Wednesday. Birthday cards in the rack by the door — paid for honestly, please.

Tins, jars & the dinner-finishers
stocked deep

Beans, soup, tinned tomatoes, pasta, rice, sauces, that one stock cube you ran out of. The cupboard staples a Sunday tea is built on.

Sweets, ice & the after-school stuff
by the till

The bottom-shelf classics, freezer-pops in summer, a 10p mix for whoever's got the right change. Coffee for the walk back.

Ask-for-it
any day

If it's not on the shelf and you'd like it on the shelf, say. It'll usually be in by the next delivery.

Built for a five-minute walk,
not a weekly shop.

The big supermarket has its place. So does the corner shop — and the corner shop's place is the bit of your week the big shop doesn't catch. A pint of milk on a Tuesday night. A loaf of bread before breakfast. A bunch of bananas because you forgot. A card for the birthday you forgot harder.

We try to keep it stocked for that. Small order, often. Honest pricing. A door that doesn't pretend to know you better than it does, but remembers your order when it sees you twice.

Of the street, by the street.

Abberley Greens sits where Stoke-on-Trent slows down a bit — in the bit of the city where people still walk to the shop, push a buggy across the road for it, send the kids up with a fiver and a list.

That's the kind of shop we try to be. Stocked for the people who live in shouting distance, not for a passing trade. If you live nearby, come and tell us what you'd like in. It's how the shelves get sharper.

"A good corner shop is a small civic thing. It opens early, it remembers, and it takes the strain off the weeks that go a bit sideways." — our brief, to ourselves
